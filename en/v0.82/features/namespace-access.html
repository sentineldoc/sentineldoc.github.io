Limiting an Account to Namespaces

Overview​

An account can be limited to some namespaces: it sees and manages only what is in them. This can be set when the account is created or later.

The role still applies inside the namespaces: a limited Admin creates and edits there, a limited Viewer reads. The same holds for the roles of your own: inside its namespaces a limited account creates and edits what its role manages and reads what its role views, see Roles and Permissions. Where this page says "limited Admin", with a role of your own it means a limited account whose role has Manage on that area.

A division of work inside a team, not isolation between tenants

The limit is enforced by the server on every request and event stream, not by hiding pages. But the Network Topology shows the names of every namespace and workload, so this is suited to dividing work inside a team, not to isolating tenants that do not trust each other.

Setting the Access​

When Creating an Account​

Under Settings → Users, click + New User, choose Selected namespaces under Access and tick the namespaces. At least one namespace has to be ticked, or the account cannot be created.

For an Existing Account​

In the user list, click Access on the account, choose Whole cluster or Selected namespaces, then Save.

  • The change takes effect at once, including in a session the account already has open; no new login is needed
  • A limited account's namespaces are shown as badges beside its name in the user list
  • An account cannot limit itself, so one that manages the others always remains

What a Limited Account Sees​

PageA limited account
Tracing Policy, Network PolicyPolicies in its namespaces only. Cluster-wide policies are neither listed nor writable
Behavior Discovery, Security Events, Admission Events, Vulnerabilities (Workloads), QuarantineIts namespaces only
Network TopologyWhole, since a connection has two ends and the far one is seldom its own. Quarantine from the topology works on its own pods only
TemplatesRead, and used to make policies. Adding, editing and deleting templates is for an Admin of the whole cluster, since they are shared by everyone
Admission PolicyA limited Admin makes policies and bindings for its own namespaces; see the next section
Registries, Event Sources, Audit Log, all of Settings, the Set all to Monitoring button on the Tracing Policy listHidden, and refused by the API. Notifications (Alerts, Syslog) and System belong to the cluster and stay out of reach of a limited account whatever its role

Further notes:

  • An event caused by a cluster-wide policy on one of its pods is shown, with the policy's name
  • A limited account's sidebar holds only Dashboard, Network Topology, Vulnerabilities → Workloads, Policies and Notifications (Security Events, Admission Events); the header does not show the "N / M in Protect" badge either. Entries the role cannot view are absent as well
  • Opening the address of a hidden page directly sends a limited account back to the Dashboard
  • The namespace filter on the Tracing Policy and Network Policy lists does not offer Cluster-wide to a limited account

Admission Policies of a Limited Admin​

Kubernetes has no namespaced admission policy, so K8s Sentinel marks each policy a limited Admin makes with the namespaces it is for (the sentinel.io/namespaces annotation) and holds it to them:

LimitDescription
The binding must be "Only these namespaces"And only among the account's own. All namespaces, All except, and selecting by any other label are refused. The binding form offers a limited account this mode only
The policy matches namespaced resources onlyscope: Namespaced is set on every rule, because a binding's namespace selector does not keep a policy off nodes, cluster roles or other cluster-scoped resources
It binds only its own policiesNot the cluster's, and it cannot change or delete a binding the cluster's Admin made for its namespace
Parameters are refusedExcept the scan counts the vulnerability rules read. A policy could otherwise be written to read out any object in a refusal message
A name some binding already refers to cannot be usedWhen the name is taken by a policy or binding the account cannot see, the message says that it already exists and to choose another

Who sees these policies:

  • A limited account lists only policies marked for namespaces that are all its own; accounts of the same namespaces share them
  • A new policy is marked for all the namespaces its author has, so an account with only some of them does not see it
  • An Admin of the whole cluster sees everything, with the namespaces shown beside the name; editing such a policy keeps the mark and the narrowing
Handing a policy to the cluster

To turn such a policy into an ordinary cluster policy, remove the mark:

kubectl annotate validatingadmissionpolicy <name> sentinel.io/namespaces-
Bindings made by an Admin of the whole cluster are not covered

A binding an Admin of the whole cluster limits to namespaces still applies its policy to cluster-scoped resources everywhere when the policy matches them (Apply To: All).

Accounts and Passwords​

  • A limited Admin cannot manage accounts or reset another account's password. Resetting another account's password takes an Admin of the whole cluster
  • A limited account, like a Viewer, has no place in the UI to change its own password; an Admin of the whole cluster resets it

Known Limits​

  • A limited account does not see the event source warnings on the Dashboard
  • Upgrading to v0.82 needs nothing beyond the new image; no manifest has to be re-applied
